# AL BASHIR, Ph.D.
I am a graduate research assistant in the Department of Bio. and Ag. Engineering at Texas A&M University, College Station, TX, USA. I recently defended my doctoral dissertation. I received my B.Sc. and M.Sc. degrees in Computer Science and Engineering from Jagannath University, Bangladesh. My research focuses on computer vision, robot perception, and learning-based control and manipulation, particularly developing algorithms that enable robots to estimate object geometry and pose, perceive their surroundings, and reliably interact with complex environments.

In my doctoral research, I have explored several problems in robotic perception and manipulation, using autonomous harvesting as a real-world testbed. My work includes RGB-D perception and target localization under occlusion, deep learning-based object detection and segmentation, multi-instance pose estimation, vision-guided robotic manipulation, and hardware design and fabrication. I have also investigated deep reinforcement learning (DRL) for robot motion control, including simulation-trained policies in NVIDIA Isaac Sim/Lab, sim-to-real transfer, and constraint-aware motion control. These efforts have involved developing perception algorithms, designing robotic end-effectors, and integrating learning-based approaches with physical robotic systems.

Building on this work, I am currently pursuing multiple projects involving vision-language-action (VLA) models and world-action models (WAM) for robot control. These projects extend my research toward embodied AI, particularly the integration of perception, prediction, and action for more generalizable robotic behavior. I am interested in developing learning-based frameworks that enable robots to reason about their environments, anticipate the consequences of their actions, and adapt to unfamiliar tasks and situations. My broader research interests include general-purpose embodied intelligence, autonomous manipulation, and perception-driven real-time control and navigation for safe and reliable robotic autonomy.
